# Constructive Theory of Reason

**A structured exploration of awareness, cognition, knowledge, learning, and practical wisdom**

This repository documents an evolving conceptual inquiry into how a biological human being becomes aware of information, develops understanding, forms usable knowledge, learns beyond what is already known, and applies and communicates what has been learned in changing circumstances.

The working framework distinguishes five stages:

1. **Awareness** (*Agahi / آگاهی*)
2. **Cognition** (*Shenakht / شناخت*)
3. **Knowledge** (*Danayi / دانایی*)
4. **Learning** (*Yad / یاد*)
5. **Civic/Practical Wisdom** (*Shahragahi / شهرآگاهی*)

These labels are working English equivalents. The Persian terms remain important because translation may not preserve every conceptual distinction.

## Purpose

The purpose of this project is to make the conceptual framework, its reasoning, its revisions, and its unresolved questions available for independent examination.

The project does not ask readers to accept the framework as established science. It invites critical assessment: whether the concepts are sufficiently distinct, whether the proposed relations are coherent, what explanatory value they may have, and what further philosophical, cognitive-scientific, or empirical work would be required.

## A preliminary structural proposition

The current working proposition distinguishes two levels of organization:

- **Within a particular dimension of a subject:** the five stages have an ordered progression.
- **Across different dimensions of a subject:** activity may be parallel, recursive, and mutually informing rather than one single, universal linear sequence.

The framework also treats the fifth stage as more demanding than possession of information alone. It includes the ability to apply, adapt, and appropriately explain what has been learned to other people. It may therefore occur by degrees rather than as a simple all-or-nothing state.

These statements summarize the current direction of the inquiry. The complete Persian source manuscript is now available in [`docs/FRAMEWORK-PERSIAN.md`](docs/FRAMEWORK-PERSIAN.md).

## Scope and limitations

- The present inquiry is grounded in human biological cognition. It does **not** claim that the same definitions automatically apply to artificial intelligence, machines, or other non-biological systems.
- The framework is conceptual and exploratory. No claim of experimental validation, clinical applicability, or scientific consensus is made.
- Terms and definitions may be revised as objections are examined and distinctions are clarified.
- The framework is not presented as a new foundation of BAES. This repository is an independent intellectual project. Any conceptual relationship to BAES must be assessed separately and must not be assumed.

## How to examine this work

Readers are encouraged to challenge:

- whether each stage has a distinct and usable definition;
- whether the proposed transition conditions are clear;
- whether the distinction between within-dimension sequence and cross-dimension recursion is coherent;
- whether the fifth stage is defined too broadly, too narrowly, or ambiguously;
- which claims are philosophical proposals and which would require empirical investigation.

Substantive criticism is welcome. The quality of the framework should be judged by the clarity of its concepts and the strength of its reasoning, not by claims made on its behalf.
