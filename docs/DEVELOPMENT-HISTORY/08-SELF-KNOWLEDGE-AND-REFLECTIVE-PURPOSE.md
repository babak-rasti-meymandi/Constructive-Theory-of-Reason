# Decision Record 08: Self-Knowledge and the Reflective Purpose of the Framework

**Project:** Constructive Theory of Reason  
**Status:** Accepted conceptual direction  
**Primary discussion language:** Persian

## 1. Context

The discussion considered whether the five stages should be operationalized through formal entry and stabilization requirements. The user clarified that the framework is not intended to issue certificates, confer qualifications, or determine an individual's official standing. Its central purpose is self-knowledge and conscious cognitive development.

The framework should help a person understand their cognitive position in relation to a particular subject or dimension, identify existing knowledge and capabilities, recognize what remains unknown, and consider what capacities could be developed next.

## 2. Accepted purpose

The framework is intended to support subject-specific self-understanding and reflective growth. It is not a credentialing, certification, employment-ranking, or social-classification system.

The stages describe a person's current development of thought relative to a specified subject or dimension. They are not fixed identity labels or unified personality traits.

## 3. Accepted self-reflection questions

The framework should help an individual ask:

- اکنون درباره یک موضوع در چه وضعیتی از رشد شناختی قرار دارد؟
- چه چیزهایی را می‌شناسد؟
- چه قابلیت‌هایی کسب کرده است؟
- چه چیزهایی را هنوز نمی‌داند؟
- برای رشد بیشتر چه ظرفیت‌هایی باید توسعه پیدا کنند؟

These are reflective questions for self-assessment, not a formal scoring or certification instrument.

## 4. Non-punitive and evidence-sensitive description

Descriptions should distinguish lack of demonstrated evidence from lack of ability. For example:

> در این موضوع، ظرفیت تعمیم را کسب کرده‌ای؛ اما هنوز فرصت یا شواهد کافی برای تحقق عملی آن در شرایط جدید نداری.

A person may possess a capacity without having had a suitable opportunity, tool, or environment to demonstrate it. Absence of demonstration must not automatically be interpreted as absence of capacity.

Self-assessment should remain revisable as the person gains experience, encounters new conditions, or obtains better evidence.

## 5. Implications for further development

- Prefer descriptive criteria and reflective prompts over formal entry, promotion, or stabilization rules.
- Use repetition, continuity, and performance across different conditions as aids to self-assessment and confidence calibration, not as administrative requirements.
- Preserve the subject-oriented nature of stage descriptions.
- Avoid global labels, personal ranking, or treating a stage as a permanent trait.
- Keep empirical assessment as a possible research activity, distinct from the framework's primary self-knowledge purpose.

## 6. Scope

This record documents a conceptual decision about the intended use of the framework. It does not claim that the reflective questions are a validated psychological instrument or that the framework has empirical validation.
