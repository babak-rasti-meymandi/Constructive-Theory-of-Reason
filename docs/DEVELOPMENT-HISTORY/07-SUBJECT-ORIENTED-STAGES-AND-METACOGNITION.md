# Decision Record 07: Subject-Oriented Stages and Metacognitive Judgment

**Project:** Constructive Theory of Reason  
**Status:** Accepted conceptual decisions; operational implications remain open  
**Primary discussion language:** Persian

## 1. Context

The discussion considered whether a person who has less knowledge about a subject but readily admits what they do not know should be regarded as cognitively further developed than a person who has substantially more knowledge and predictive understanding but is reluctant to accept limitations in their own model.

The comparison was deliberately about a particular subject, not a global ranking of the two people's personalities or general worth.

A philosophical saying recalled during the discussion — “دانا کسی هست که می‌داند که نمی‌داند” (“The wise/knowledgeable person is one who knows that they do not know”) — prompted examination of the relation between knowledge, awareness of limitations, and metacognition. Its historical attribution was not investigated in this discussion.

## 2. Accepted decision: Person 1 is higher in the specified subject

The two cases were:

- **Person 1:** has more knowledge, understands more of the subject's relations, and can make predictions; however, they are reluctant to accept limitations in their own cognitive model. They have nevertheless left open the possibility of acceptance and revision.
- **Person 2:** has less knowledge, but readily admits what they do not know and is more cautious about their own claims.

**The user's final decision is that Person 1 is at the higher cognitive stage in relation to that subject.**

The rationale is that greater subject-specific cognitive development — including connected understanding of relations and predictive capacity — carries greater weight in determining the stage than ease of admitting ignorance alone. Person 1's reluctance to accept limitations is relevant to the quality of judgment, but the fact that the path to acceptance and revision remains open means it does not, by itself, erase the more advanced subject-specific understanding.

Person 2's epistemic humility is valuable, but it does not substitute for the knowledge and relational understanding that distinguish the higher stages.

This decision does not endorse rigidity or resistance to evidence. It distinguishes the stage attained from the quality and calibration with which the person exercises that cognition.

## 3. Accepted general principle: stages are subject-oriented

The user explicitly established:

> **مراحل توسعه تفکر موضوع‌محورند، نه ویژگی ثابت و یکپارچه شخصیت افراد.**

In English:

> **The stages of thought development are subject-oriented, not fixed and unified personality traits.**

Consequences:

- A stage attribution must identify the subject or dimension being assessed.
- One person may be at different stages across different subjects.
- Even within one broad subject, a person may be at different stages in distinct dimensions.
- The framework must not use the five stages as global personality labels or as a single, unified ranking of people.

This principle applies to all five stages, not only to *شهرآگاهی*.

## 4. Relation to metacognition

The discussion identified a possible relation between cognitive development and the ability to recognize the limits of one's own knowledge.

The following distinctions should be preserved:

- Admitting “I do not know” is not, by itself, sufficient evidence of a higher cognitive stage.
- More developed understanding of a subject may enable a person to identify more precisely what is known, unknown, uncertain, or not yet tested.
- Openness to revision is relevant. A person who is reluctant but has not closed the possibility of correction is not equivalent to a person who categorically rejects revision.
- Resistance to revision can impair judgment quality and confidence calibration without automatically determining the person's stage in every dimension.

The exact operational relationship between metacognitive calibration, knowledge, and stage attainment remains open.

## 5. Questions retained for further work

1. How should subject-specific cognitive stage and metacognitive calibration be assessed as distinct but interacting dimensions?
2. What observable evidence distinguishes a person who is merely willing to admit ignorance from one who can identify the structure and boundaries of their own knowledge?
3. How should the framework classify a person whose subject-specific knowledge is advanced but whose willingness to revise is weak, while not entirely absent?
4. What threshold would indicate that resistance to revision has become so strong that it undermines the person's claimed predictive understanding or the evidence for their stage?

## 6. Status and scope

The decisions in this record are accepted into the current conceptual framework. They are not claims of empirical validation or scientific consensus. Operational criteria and assessment methods remain to be developed.
