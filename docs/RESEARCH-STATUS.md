# Research Status

**Project:** Constructive Theory of Reason  
**Status:** Conceptual framework under consolidation  
**Primary language of the source discussion:** Persian

## Current working structure

The discussion has developed a five-stage framework:

1. Awareness — آگاهی
2. Cognition — شناخت
3. Knowledge — دانایی
4. Learning — یاد
5. *شهرآگاهی* — provisional English rendering: Civic/Practical Wisdom

The five stages are intended to have distinct conditions of attainment. They should not be collapsed into interchangeable labels for general intelligence or accumulated information.

## Positions explicitly reached in the discussion

- Awareness, in the current biological framing, involves recognizing novelty or difference in relation to memory; mere signal registration is not sufficient.
- Cognition concerns the development and interpretation of what has entered awareness.
- Knowledge (دانایی) includes connected, multi-aspect understanding and the use of known relations to predict behavior and analyze or control purposeful changes. A successful prediction in an unfamiliar case can still be an application of an existing model within دانایی.
- Learning (یاد) requires categorization and pattern recognition with sufficient accuracy, applicability, and generalizability to reconstruct understanding and apply extracted patterns in genuinely new conditions or structurally related domains. Generalization within a domain can be sufficient; transfer to an unrelated domain is not mandatory.
- A “new capability” in یاد means a capability newly acquired by the individual through generalization and reconstruction of understanding. World-first invention or discovery is not required.
- *شهرآگاهی* concerns adaptive, goal-directed use, integration, and revision of knowledge and skills in a changing real environment. Appropriate explanation to others remains a necessary condition in the current framework.
- Invention and/or discovery are not conditions for attaining *شهرآگاهی*. They are quality and degree indicators; an actual invention or discovery is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself.
- *شهرآگاهی* is a matter of degree; it should not be treated as a binary label.
- Importance is in a reciprocal relation with cognitive growth: needs, motivations, and importance assessments direct and modulate cognition, while cognitive growth improves importance evaluation, priority revision, and consequence recognition. Importance is neither an absolute prerequisite nor merely a final product of reason.
- Within a particular dimension of a subject, the five stages are ordered. Across different dimensions, their operation may be parallel and recursive.
- Supporting processes such as memory, attention, processing, comparison, inference, evaluation, judgment, choice, and decision are not additional primary stages in the model.

## Scope boundary

The current definitions are framed around biological human cognition. The framework has not established that the same concepts or criteria apply to artificial intelligence or non-biological systems. Such an extension must not be implied by the current material.

## What this repository does not claim

At this stage, the repository does not claim:

- empirical or experimental validation;
- acceptance by a scientific or philosophical community;
- a complete account of human cognition;
- a settled English translation for every Persian term;
- that the five stages are universally linear across all cognitive dimensions;
- that the framework is a BAES foundation or has been validated by BAES.

## Publication principle

The repository should preserve the difference between:

- a position explicitly reached in the dialogue;
- a provisional formulation;
- an interpretation or editorial clarification;
- an unresolved question;
- a claim that would require external or empirical evidence.

A lack of evidence should be reported as a limitation, not filled with speculation.

## Additional conceptual decisions: subject orientation and metacognitive comparison

- **The stages of thought development are subject-oriented, not fixed and unified personality traits.** A person's stage must be assessed relative to a specified subject or dimension. The same person may occupy different stages across different subjects or dimensions of one subject.
- In a comparison concerning a particular subject, greater subject-specific knowledge, relational understanding, and predictive capacity can place Person 1 at a higher cognitive stage than Person 2, even if Person 1 is reluctant to accept the limitations of their model, provided that the possibility of revision and acceptance remains open.
- Person 2's lower knowledge combined with easier admission of not knowing does not, by itself, place Person 2 at a higher stage. Epistemic humility and openness to correction matter, but they do not substitute for the development of subject-specific understanding.
- Resistance to revision may reduce the quality and calibration of judgment; it is not automatically equivalent to absence of advanced cognition when the possibility of revision remains open.

These are conceptual decisions reached in the discussion. Their implications for assessment and the relation between metacognitive calibration and stage attainment remain open for further operational work.
