# Framework Overview

**Status: preliminary conceptual overview**

This document records the current high-level structure of the five-stage framework. It is deliberately concise. It does not replace the full Persian discussion or its detailed question-and-answer development.

## 1. Awareness — آگاهی

The current formulation concerns the recognition of something as new or different in relation to what is already retained in memory.

The distinction is important: the mere registration of a physical signal is not, by itself, sufficient to establish awareness in the sense used by this framework. For example, a Geiger counter registering radiation is not thereby an aware subject.

**Working question:** What minimum conditions distinguish awareness from mere signal detection or registration?

## 2. Cognition — شناخت

Cognition concerns the development and organization of the content that has entered awareness. It is not simply the occurrence of a new signal; it involves working out what the information means or how it relates to what is already known.

**Working question:** What conditions mark the transition from becoming aware of something to recognizing and understanding it?

## 3. Knowledge — دانایی

Knowledge concerns information and understanding that have become sufficiently usable to support judgment or prediction. In the current discussion, the ability to anticipate an outcome on the basis of what is known is an important indicator.

**Working question:** What distinguishes knowledge from familiarity, memorization, or an untested belief?

## 4. Learning — یاد

Learning extends beyond applying an existing body of knowledge. The current formulation includes the ability to generalize, discover relationships, identify patterns, anticipate needs, and produce a new understanding or solution.

The development of a useful tool from accumulated experience is one example discussed in the project.

**Working question:** What distinguishes learning in this stronger sense from routine acquisition or repetition?

## 5. Civic/Practical Wisdom — شهرآگاهی

The fifth stage concerns the integration, adaptation, and appropriate use of knowledge and learned capabilities in a changing environment in pursuit of a goal. The current discussion emphasizes that a person may reach a goal through more than one possible route and must be able to respond when circumstances change.

An additional condition was explicitly emphasized: a person cannot reasonably claim to be *شهرآگاه* in a subject merely by privately possessing information or skill. They must also be able to explain what they know appropriately to other people. This capability can exist at different degrees.

The English label **Civic/Practical Wisdom** is provisional. It should not be treated as a settled translation of *شهرآگاهی*.

**Working questions:**
- What makes an explanation appropriate for another person and context?
- How should degrees of this capability be distinguished?
- How do adaptation, goal-directed action, and communication relate to one another?

## 6. Structural relationship among the stages

The current position distinguishes the scale at which sequence is being considered:

- **Within one defined dimension of a subject:** the progression is ordered — Awareness → Cognition → Knowledge → Learning → *شهرآگاهی*.
- **Across different dimensions of a subject:** the processes may operate in parallel and may recur or feed back into one another.

This distinction avoids treating the entire cognitive life of a person as one simple, globally linear chain.

## 7. Supporting processes

The discussion has also considered supporting processes such as memory, attention, processing, comparison, inference, evaluation, judgment, choice, and decision.

At this stage, these are treated as supporting mechanisms, not as additional primary stages in the five-stage sequence.

## 8. Important boundary

The definitions in this overview are currently framed around biological human cognition. Extending them to artificial intelligence, non-biological tools, or other systems would require a separate argument. Such an extension is not established by this framework overview.
